Lease Rental Discounting Is Not Simply a Loan Against Property

A traditional property-backed loan may primarily focus on the value of the underlying asset. Lease Rental Discounting adds another dimension. It focuses on the quality and predictability of the rental cash flows generated by the property.

An office building occupied by a strong corporate tenant under a long-term lease presents a different financing proposition from a vacant building of similar market value. The lender is not evaluating only what is the property worth? The lender is also asking:

  • Who pays the rent?
  • How much rent is contracted?
  • How long will the lease continue?
  • Is the tenant locked in?
  • How secure is the cash flow?
  • Can rental collections service the proposed debt?

This makes LRD a cash-flow-underwriting transaction supported by commercial real estate. EquiFin benchmarks LRD as yield-driven financing backed by stable contracted commercial real estate cash flows, with focus on WALE, NOI, lock-in, escrow and tenant covenant quality.

How a Leased Asset Becomes Financeable

The financing logic behind an LRD transaction can be understood through a simple sequence.

Step 1

The Property Is Leased

The commercial asset has one or more tenants — office occupiers, IT companies, MNCs, industrial tenants, warehousing operators or retail brands.

Step 2

Lease Creates Contracted Rental Obligations

The lease agreement defines commercial terms such as monthly rent, lease tenure, lock-in period, escalation clauses, security deposits and termination provisions. These terms determine the visibility of future rental cash flows.

Step 3

Rental Income Becomes the Primary Cash Flow

The property owner receives recurring lease rentals. The lender evaluates whether these rentals provide sufficient and reliable debt servicing capacity.

Step 4

Rentals Are Structured Through an Escrow

In many LRD structures, rental collections may flow through an agreed escrow mechanism. The lender obtains greater visibility over the cash flow.

Step 5

Debt Is Serviced From Rental Cash Flows

The financing structure is designed around:

Contracted Rental Income → Escrow → Debt Servicing

The Core Financing Insight — the value of the property provides security. But the quality of contracted rent provides repayment visibility.

A ₹500 Crore Property Does Not Automatically Support ₹500 Crore of Financing

Property value alone does not determine debt capacity. Consider two commercial assets.

Asset A

  • Strong MNC tenant
  • Long unexpired lease
  • Healthy lock-in period
  • Stable rental collections
  • Escrow visibility

Asset B

  • Multiple weak tenants
  • Short lease expiries
  • High vacancy probability
  • Uncertain renewals
  • Irregular collections

Both assets may have similar property valuations. But their financing profiles can be materially different. Why? Because LRD lenders underwrite:

Property + Tenant + Lease + Cash Flow — not property value in isolation.

Four Layers of LRD Underwriting

A useful EquiFin framework:

Layer 1 — The Asset

What is the underlying property? Office, IT Park, industrial facility, warehouse, retail asset or mixed-use commercial development.

Layer 2 — The Tenant

Who generates the rental cash flow? MNC, listed corporate, Grade-A Indian corporate, institutional tenant or SME occupier. Tenant covenant quality can materially influence lender appetite.

Layer 3 — The Lease

How durable is the contractual relationship? Lenders review remaining lease tenure, lock-in period, escalation clauses, renewal provisions and termination rights.

Layer 4 — The Cash Flow

Can rental income support debt servicing? This is where metrics such as NOI, DSCR, rental yield and debt service become important.

The strongest LRD transaction sits at the intersection of a good asset, credible tenant, durable lease and visible rental cash flow.

Tenant Quality

In LRD, the Tenant Can Be as Important as the Property

The tenant is the source of the rental cash flow. Therefore, lenders often assess the tenant's ability and willingness to continue meeting lease obligations. Potential considerations include:

  • Corporate credit profile
  • Financial strength
  • Market position
  • Business stability
  • Operating history
  • Industry outlook

A Grade-A corporate tenant with a long-term occupation strategy can provide greater confidence around future rentals than a financially weak or unstable tenant. This does not mean smaller tenants are automatically unfinanceable — it means the lender's underwriting will place greater emphasis on cash-flow predictability, diversification, vacancy risk and lease security.

Tenant credit profiles — including MNCs and Grade-A corporates — are a primary LRD underwriting parameter.

Lease Tenure vs Loan Tenure

The Debt Should Not Outlive the Visibility of the Cash Flow

One of the most important structuring questions in LRD is: how long is the rental income contractually visible compared with the proposed debt repayment period?

Scenario A

Remaining Lease Tenure: 12 Years, Proposed Loan Tenure: 8 Years

The contracted cash-flow horizon extends beyond the proposed debt period.

Scenario B

Remaining Lease Tenure: 3 Years, Proposed Loan Tenure: 10 Years

The lender faces greater uncertainty. What happens after the lease expires? Will the tenant renew? At what rental rate? Will there be vacancy?

Why WALE Matters — Weighted Average Lease Expiry (WALE) provides a view of the weighted average remaining lease duration across multiple tenants. For multi-tenant assets, WALE helps answer: how long is the existing rental income expected to remain contractually visible?

The longer the visible lease horizon relative to debt obligations, the stronger the lender's cash-flow visibility may be. WALE and remaining lease tenure versus proposed debt tenure are central LRD underwriting considerations.

Lock-In Period

A Lease Agreement Is Not Always the Same as Cash-Flow Certainty

A lease may have a long overall tenure. But the tenant may have rights to terminate under certain conditions. This makes the lock-in period important. The lock-in period provides a stronger contractual commitment for a defined period, subject to the lease terms.

Lenders may therefore distinguish between:

  • Total lease tenure
  • Remaining lock-in
  • Notice period
  • Termination clauses

The practical question: how much of the future rental stream is contractually protected against early tenant exit? A long lease with weak termination protection can present a different risk profile from a shorter lease with strong lock-in provisions.

Rent Escalation

Future Rental Growth Can Influence Cash-Flow Visibility

Commercial leases may include periodic rent escalation clauses. For example, rental income may increase at agreed intervals. This can potentially improve future debt service capacity. However, lenders typically need to distinguish between:

Existing Contracted Rent

Already payable under the lease.

Future Contractual Escalation

Scheduled increase under existing terms.

Market Rental Assumptions

Potential future rent based on expected market conditions.

The strongest financing assumptions are generally those supported by contractual visibility rather than speculative rental growth.

Escrow Mechanism

Why Rental Escrow Matters to Lenders

An LRD transaction is fundamentally dependent on rental cash flows. The lender therefore seeks visibility over where those rentals are received and how they are applied. A simplified structure can look like:

Tenant → Monthly Lease Rental → Escrow Account

  1. Debt Servicing
  2. Interest Payment
  3. Principal Repayment

Residual Cash Flow → Property Owner

An escrow mechanism can help create a structured payment waterfall. Direct escrow of rentals is a key security and risk-mitigation structure in LRD underwriting.

The Lender's Perspective — the lender wants to reduce uncertainty around where rental cash is received, whether debt servicing has priority, and whether collections can be monitored.

Tripartite Agreements

Aligning the Property Owner, Tenant and Lender

Depending on transaction structure, a tripartite arrangement may be used to establish communication and acknowledgement among relevant parties. The structure can help clarify matters relating to:

  • Rental payment instructions
  • Escrow arrangements
  • Lender rights
  • Notice requirements

The exact legal structure depends on the transaction and documentation.

EquiFin Perspective

In LRD, documentation is not only a legal exercise. It is part of the cash-flow control architecture.

Tripartite agreements are a core semantic and structural element of LRD transactions.

How Do Lenders Actually Evaluate an LRD Transaction?

The credit committee looks beyond the property valuation.

1. Tenant Covenant Quality

Who is paying the rent? The lender evaluates corporate profile, financial strength, business continuity and payment history. The strength of the tenant covenant can materially influence financing appetite.

2. Remaining Lease Tenure

How long will the current lease continue? A lender generally seeks alignment between cash-flow visibility and proposed loan tenure.

3. WALE

For multi-tenant properties: how concentrated or diversified are lease expiries? A property where multiple major tenants expire simultaneously may carry greater refinancing or vacancy risk.

4. Lock-In & Termination Clauses

Can tenants exit early? The lender evaluates contractual protection around lock-in, break clauses, notice periods and termination rights.

5. Rental Cash Flow

The lender analyses gross rentals, net rentals, escalation and collection history. The focus is on sustainable cash flow.

6. Net Operating Income (NOI)

NOI represents the income generated by the property after relevant operating expenses, before financing costs. A lender may analyse whether the property's operating income provides sufficient coverage for proposed debt servicing.

7. DSCR

DSCR = Cash Available for Debt Service ÷ Total Debt Service

Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) measures the relationship between available cash flow and debt servicing obligations. For LRD, minimum DSCR expectations are broadly in the region of approximately 1.20x–1.30x on escrowed rentals, subject to transaction and lender assessment. A DSCR above 1.0x means the relevant cash flow exceeds scheduled debt service — higher coverage provides a greater cushion against stress.

8. Loan-to-Value (LTV)

Lenders also evaluate the financing amount relative to property value. Typical LRD underwriting ranges are approximately 65%–75% LTV, depending on asset, tenant, transaction structure and lender policy. Property value remains important, but in LRD: asset value protects downside, rental cash flow supports repayment.

9. Vacancy Risk

What happens if a tenant leaves? The lender considers replacement tenant potential, micro-market demand, asset quality, location and re-leasing period. Vacancy can immediately affect debt service visibility.

A useful EquiFin visual framework:

How Much Debt Can a Leased Property Support?

LRD Debt Sizing Is Usually a Cash-Flow Exercise Before It Is a Property Valuation Exercise

The question property owners frequently ask is: how much loan can I obtain against my rental property? There is no single answer. Debt capacity can be influenced by multiple approaches.

Approach 1 — Rental Income Multiple

Owners often evaluate LRD quantum against annual net lease income, with indicative market positioning sometimes expressed as a multiple of net annual rentals. However, the applicable multiple can vary significantly based on tenant quality, lease tenure, interest rates, repayment structure and lender appetite.

Approach 2 — Discounted Rental Cash Flows

The lender may evaluate the present value of visible future rental cash flows. Longer and more secure contractual cash flows can create stronger debt capacity.

Approach 3 — DSCR-Based Sizing

The debt may be sized so that expected rental cash flows maintain sufficient coverage over interest and principal obligations.

Approach 4 — LTV Constraint

Even where rental cash flows support a higher loan amount, property value and LTV policy may create an additional constraint.

The Final Debt Quantum — in practice, the financeable amount may be constrained by the most conservative of:

Cash Flow Capacity, Lease Visibility, Property Value, Lender Policy

Single-Tenant vs Multi-Tenant LRD

Different tenant structures create different credit profiles.

Single-Tenant Asset

Advantages: simple cash-flow structure, easier monitoring, clear tenant covenant.

Risk: complete dependence on one tenant. If the tenant exits, rental income can fall significantly.

Multi-Tenant Asset

Advantages: diversified rental base, lower dependence on a single tenant.

Challenges: different lease expiries, different rental rates, multiple renewals, tenant turnover. WALE becomes particularly important.

EquiFin Perspective

Diversification reduces concentration risk — but increases lease management complexity.

Mixed-Use Commercial Developments

One Property Can Contain Multiple Cash-Flow Profiles

Mixed-use assets may combine office, retail, food & beverage, warehousing and commercial services. The challenge is that each segment may have different tenant profiles, different lease tenures, different vacancy behaviour and different rental yields.

A lender may therefore need to analyse whether the asset should be viewed as one combined cash-flow pool or multiple distinct rental segments. Mixed-use commercial developments remain an underexplored LRD structuring opportunity.

LRD for Industrial & Warehousing Assets

Industrial Real Estate Can Have a Different Underwriting Logic

For warehouses and industrial assets, tenant quality remains important. But lenders may also consider asset location, connectivity, industrial corridor relevance, replacement demand and specialised infrastructure.

LRD structures for logistics and warehousing often include direct rental escrow, tenant agreements and property-level security. A logistics asset occupied by a long-term institutional tenant may therefore present a strong cash-flow financing case. However, lenders still need to understand: how easily can the asset be re-leased if the current tenant exits?

Upcoming Lease Expiry

What Happens When the Lease Expires Before the Loan?

This is one of the most important practical issues in LRD. A property may have good current rent, a strong tenant and high current occupancy — but an upcoming lease expiry. The lender must then assess future uncertainty. Questions include:

  • Is renewal likely?
  • What is the tenant's strategic importance to the location?
  • Is market rent higher or lower?
  • How long might re-leasing take?

A good LRD structure does not ignore future expiry risk. It asks: what happens to debt servicing if today's tenant does not remain tomorrow? Possible structuring considerations may include:

  • Shorter debt tenor
  • Amortisation alignment
  • Additional DSCR cushion
  • Conservative debt sizing
  • Refinancing strategy

Refinancing an Existing LRD

The Existing Loan Structure May No Longer Be Optimal

Commercial property owners may review refinancing when interest costs have changed, rental income has increased, new leases have been executed, property value has improved, or existing loan terms are restrictive. A refinancing transaction may potentially seek to:

  • Improve pricing
  • Extend maturity
  • Restructure amortisation
  • Release surplus capital
  • Consolidate facilities

However, refinancing should not be viewed simply as moving the loan to another lender. The new lender will re-evaluate:

Asset + Tenant + Lease + Cash Flow

Interest Rate & Refinancing Risk

A Stable Rental Asset Can Still Face Financing Risk

Even when rental income remains stable, financing costs can change. This can affect interest coverage, DSCR, cash distributions and refinancing capacity. A prudent financing analysis should therefore consider:

Base Case

Current rental and interest assumptions.

Stress Case

Potential increase in interest rates or reduction in rental income.

Lease Expiry Case

Potential vacancy or delay in renewal.

The objective is to understand whether the financing structure remains sustainable beyond the base-case scenario.

Balloon Amortisation

Lower Periodic Repayment Can Create a Future Refinancing Event

Some financing structures may include balloon repayment characteristics. This can reduce periodic principal amortisation. However, it may leave a significant amount of debt outstanding at maturity. The future repayment may therefore depend on asset sale, lease renewal, refinancing or internal cash reserves.

EquiFin Perspective

A lower EMI does not necessarily mean lower financing risk. The repayment profile must be evaluated together with the exit strategy.

Key Financial Metrics

Metrics that shape LRD bankability:

Net Operating Income (NOI)

Income generated by the property after relevant operating expenses and before financing costs.

DSCR

Measures the ability of rental cash flow to service debt obligations. LRD DSCR expectations are broadly around 1.20x–1.30x, depending on lender and transaction structure.

LTV

Measures proposed debt relative to property value. Typical LRD underwriting ranges are around 65%–75% LTV, subject to asset and transaction specifics.

WALE

Weighted Average Lease Expiry measures the weighted average remaining lease duration across tenants.

Occupancy

Measures the proportion of the asset currently generating rental income.

Rental Yield

Provides a relationship between rental income and property value. However, yield alone does not capture tenant and lease quality.

When LRD May Not Be the Right Structure

Not Every Commercial Property Is an LRD Candidate

LRD may be less suitable where:

  • The asset has high vacancy
  • Lease tenure is very short
  • Tenant credit quality is weak
  • Rental income is inconsistent
  • Major lease expiries are imminent
  • Property documentation is incomplete

Similarly, a property with strong market value but weak contracted rental visibility may not support the same financing structure as a stabilised leased asset.

EquiFin Perspective

LRD finances visible rental cash flows. If the cash flow is uncertain, the financing structure must recognise that uncertainty rather than ignore it.

EquiFin Perspective

The objective of Lease Rental Discounting is not simply to borrow against a property. It is to convert a durable, contracted rental stream into appropriately structured capital — while preserving the long-term value and ownership of the underlying commercial asset.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Lease Rental Discounting?

Lease Rental Discounting, or LRD, is a financing structure where a commercial property owner's contracted rental income is evaluated as a source of debt repayment, with the underlying property typically forming part of the security package.

How is LRD different from a loan against property?

A conventional property-backed loan may focus primarily on asset value and borrower profile. LRD places additional emphasis on rental cash flows, tenant quality, lease tenure, WALE, lock-in and debt service coverage.

How is the LRD loan amount determined?

Debt capacity can depend on multiple factors, including contracted rentals, tenant quality, remaining lease tenure, DSCR, property value and lender-specific LTV policies.

What is WALE?

WALE stands for Weighted Average Lease Expiry. It measures the weighted average remaining lease duration across a property's tenant base and helps assess future rental cash-flow visibility.

Why is tenant quality important in LRD?

The tenant generates the rental cash flow used to support debt servicing. Stronger tenant covenant quality and stable payment history can improve lender confidence, subject to overall transaction assessment.

What is an escrow mechanism in LRD?

An escrow mechanism is used to route rental collections through a controlled account structure, helping create visibility and an agreed payment waterfall for debt servicing.

What happens if a tenant leaves before the loan is repaid?

Tenant exit can reduce rental cash flow and create vacancy risk. Lenders may evaluate lease expiry, lock-in provisions, replacement demand and debt service resilience while structuring the transaction.

Can LRD be used for refinancing?

Yes. Stabilised leased commercial assets may be evaluated for refinancing, subject to tenant profile, lease structure, rental cash flow, existing debt and lender underwriting.

What types of properties can be considered for LRD?

Depending on lender appetite, leased office buildings, IT parks, industrial assets, warehouses, retail properties and selected mixed-use commercial developments may be evaluated.

Does EquiFin provide LRD loans directly?

No. EquiFin acts as an independent financial advisory firm supporting transaction assessment, structuring and lender alignment. Financing decisions and final terms remain subject to independent lender evaluation.
