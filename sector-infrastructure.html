What Makes This Sector Different

Infrastructure capital has to survive the entire project lifecycle, not just construction. Bid-stage commitments and equity go in long before commissioning; EPC contractors carry both fund-based working capital and non-fund exposure (BGs, LCs) that grows with the order book; and a project entity's cash flow depends on the contractual framework — concession terms, revenue mechanism and counterparty — as much as on physical completion.

Capital Requirements That Typically Arise

  • Bid & Development Stage — equity and early-stage capital before financial close.
  • EPC Working Capital & Guarantees — fund-based limits plus performance and financial BGs that scale with the order book.
  • Construction / Project Debt — sized to project cost, contractual milestones and completion risk.
  • Receivables & Execution Funding — bridging the gap between certified work and actual payment.
  • Refinancing — onto structured or long-term debt once construction risk reduces and cash flow stabilises.

What Lenders Evaluate

  • Contractual Framework — concession terms, revenue mechanism and counterparty quality (including HAM-specific dynamics where relevant).
  • DSCR — cash available for debt service against scheduled obligations, sized to the asset's actual risk.
  • Execution Risk — EPC capability, right-of-way status and project cost overrun exposure.
  • Order Book Quality — a strong order book is not the same as cash flow; certification and collection timelines matter.
  • Cash-Flow Control — escrow / TRA mechanics and debt tenor aligned to the project's actual cash-flow curve.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are infrastructure projects typically financed?

Infrastructure projects may use a combination of sponsor equity, long-term project debt and transaction-specific facilities. The structure depends on project stage, contractual framework, cash-flow visibility and lender assessment of execution risk.

What do lenders evaluate in infrastructure financing?

Lenders typically examine the contractual framework, revenue mechanism, counterparty quality, EPC execution capability, sponsor contribution, project cost, completion risk and projected debt servicing capacity.

Why do EPC contractors need significant banking limits?

EPC companies may require fund-based working capital as well as non-fund facilities such as performance guarantees, financial guarantees and letters of credit. Growth in the order book can therefore increase banking capacity requirements.

When should an infrastructure asset consider refinancing?

Refinancing may become relevant after construction risk reduces and the asset establishes an operating cash-flow history. The objective is to assess whether the existing financing structure remains appropriate for the asset's current risk profile.
