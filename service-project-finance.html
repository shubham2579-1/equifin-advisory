Project Finance

Long-Term Project Capital Starts With Bankable Cash Flows.

A project’s ability to raise debt depends on more than total project cost or asset value — lenders look at how the project gets built, when it starts operating, and whether the resulting cash flows can reliably service long-term debt. EquiFin helps sponsors structure project financing around project economics, construction risk and lender underwriting requirements.

Where This Becomes Relevant

Project finance advisory tends to matter most for:

  • Greenfield Projects — no operating history to lean on, so sponsor capability, technical feasibility and revenue visibility carry the credit case.
  • Brownfield Expansion — an operating track record helps, but the incremental capacity still needs to stand on its own cash flows.
  • Infrastructure Development — long gestation periods, concession structures and counterparty-dependent revenue.
  • Renewable Energy Projects — financeability tied to PPA quality, counterparty strength and generation assumptions.
  • Industrial & Manufacturing Capex — capacity creation that needs time to ramp up to intended utilisation.

What Lenders Actually Evaluate

Project lenders underwrite the asset, not just the sponsor. They typically look at:

  • Techno-Economic Viability — whether the project’s technical configuration and cost/revenue assumptions actually hold up.
  • DSCR — minimum and average debt service coverage across the tenor, not just a headline project IRR.
  • Completion Risk — EPC credentials, contract structure, liquidated damages and contingency for delay or cost overrun.
  • Revenue & Offtake Risk — how predictable and enforceable the revenue actually is: PPA, concession, take-or-pay or merchant exposure.
  • Promoter Contribution — quantum, timing and source of sponsor equity.
  • Security & Cash-Flow Controls — charge over project assets, assignment of contracts, escrow / TRA mechanics.

How the Financing Is Approached

Completion and bankability aren’t the same thing. A project can be technically finished, fully approved and commercially attractive, and still hit a liquidity gap if production ramps up slower than the scheduled repayment assumes. We work through project cost, means of finance, drawdown against construction milestones, moratorium and amortisation before the transaction goes to a lender — asking not just when the project will be built, but when it will be able to comfortably service debt.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is project finance?

Project finance is a financing approach in which the proposed project's economics, assets and future cash flows play a central role in determining debt capacity and repayment. The structure is commonly used for capital-intensive projects with defined development, construction and operating phases.

How is project debt sized?

Project debt is typically assessed against factors such as total project cost, promoter contribution, projected cash flows and the project's ability to meet scheduled debt obligations. Coverage metrics such as DSCR may form an important part of debt sizing.

What is a DSCR in project finance?

Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) measures the relationship between cash available for debt service and scheduled principal and interest obligations. Lenders may evaluate both minimum and average DSCR over the financing period.

Does project finance guarantee that a project will receive funding?

No. EquiFin provides advisory and transaction execution support. Financing remains subject to the independent appraisal, credit processes, documentation requirements and approvals of the relevant capital providers.

A Project Requirement Is Not Yet a Financing Structure.

Before approaching lenders, the project needs a credible view of its cost, timeline, operating assumptions and debt-servicing capacity.
