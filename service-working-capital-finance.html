Working Capital Is Not a Loan Requirement. It Is an Operating Cycle Requirement.

Working capital requirements are created by the way a business operates.

A manufacturer may need to purchase raw materials months before receiving payment from customers. A distributor may carry inventory across multiple locations. An EPC contractor may incur execution costs before milestone payments are certified.

The financing requirement emerges from the gap between: when cash leaves the business and when cash returns. This is the working-capital cycle.

The financing question is therefore not simply how much working capital limit can the company obtain? It is: does the existing banking structure adequately support the way the business actually converts inventory and receivables into cash?

A well-structured working capital arrangement needs to consider:

  • Inventory cycle
  • Receivable cycle
  • Supplier credit
  • Seasonality
  • Sales growth
  • Drawing Power
  • Existing utilisation
  • Fund-based limits
  • Non-fund-based limits
  • Collateral structure

EquiFin approaches working capital as an operating liquidity architecture — not as a generic business loan.

Where Does Working Capital Actually Get Stuck?

Every operating business has a cash conversion cycle. The length and structure of that cycle determine how much external liquidity may be required.

Step 1

Cash Goes Into Procurement

The business purchases raw materials, components, trading goods or consumables. Cash leaves the business.

Step 2

Inventory Is Held

Materials may remain in warehouses, in production, in transit or as finished goods. The capital remains tied up.

Step 3

Goods or Services Are Sold

Revenue may be recognised. But revenue recognition does not necessarily mean cash has been received.

Step 4

Credit Is Extended

The customer may receive 30 days, 60 days, 90 days or longer contractual credit. The business now carries a receivable.

Step 5

Collections Arrive

Only when the receivable is collected does the operating cycle convert back into cash.

The Core Problem — if the business has to pay suppliers before collecting from customers, external working capital may be required to bridge the gap. The cycle can be expressed simply as:

Inventory Days + Receivable Days − Payable Days = Cash Conversion Cycle

The longer the cycle, the more capital can remain locked within operations.

Revenue Growth Can Increase Liquidity Pressure

Growth is not always immediately cash-generative. Consider a business whose sales increase rapidly. Higher sales may require more raw material purchases, higher inventory levels, greater production capacity utilisation and larger receivables outstanding.

The P&L may show growth. But the balance sheet may show increasing capital locked in operations. This creates a common situation:

The business is growing, but liquidity is tightening.

Working capital financing should therefore be reviewed not only when a company faces distress. It should also be reviewed when growth changes the scale of the operating cycle.

Where Businesses Typically Need Working Capital

01

Growth in Sales Requires Higher Inventory

A business may receive stronger demand and increase production. However, higher production can require larger raw material procurement, higher work-in-progress and increased finished goods inventory. The financing requirement may increase before additional sales collections arrive.

The core question: can the existing working capital limits support the next level of operating activity?

02

Receivable Days Are Increasing

A company's revenue may remain strong while collections slow. This can occur because customers negotiate longer credit, large institutional buyers have extended payment cycles, project certifications take time or disputes delay invoice settlement. The impact is immediate — cash becomes locked in debtors.

03

Seasonal Businesses Require Peak Limits

Some businesses operate with significant seasonal variation — pre-season inventory build-up, festival-led demand, agricultural cycles or annual procurement cycles. A working capital structure based only on average utilisation may become inadequate during peak periods. The requirement may need to distinguish between base working capital and seasonal working capital.

04

Fund-Based Limits Are Fully Utilised

A company may regularly operate close to Cash Credit, Overdraft or WCDL limits. High utilisation does not automatically mean that the facility is inadequate. However, persistent utilisation can indicate that the operating cycle has lengthened, sales have increased, receivables have expanded or existing limits are no longer aligned with operations. A structured limit review may therefore become relevant.

05

LC or BG Requirements Are Constraining Growth

Not all working capital requirements involve cash utilisation. A business may require non-fund facilities such as Letters of Credit, Financial Bank Guarantees or Performance Bank Guarantees. These facilities can consume banking exposure and collateral capacity. The challenge is often not simply obtaining more limits — it is balancing fund-based liquidity requirements with non-fund-based banking requirements.

06

Multiple Banks Are Creating Coordination Friction

As companies grow, they may add banking relationships. Over time, this can create multiple security arrangements, different documentation requirements, divergent covenants, coordination delays and inter-bank approvals. At this stage, working capital becomes a banking structure problem — not simply a borrowing requirement.

Fund-Based vs Non-Fund-Based Facilities

One of the biggest content gaps among competitors is that working capital products are simply listed. The more sophisticated discussion is about how fund-based and non-fund-based facilities interact within the overall capital structure.

Fund-Based Facilities

Fund-based facilities result in an actual deployment of lender funds.

Cash Credit (CC)

A revolving facility generally linked to eligible working capital assets and Drawing Power. Used for day-to-day liquidity, inventory financing and receivables financing.

Overdraft (OD)

A facility allowing a business to draw funds up to an approved limit, subject to facility terms. The structure may differ depending on the security and banking arrangement.

Working Capital Demand Loan (WCDL)

A demand loan structure that may be used alongside other working capital facilities. WCDL can help structure utilisation differently from a purely revolving Cash Credit facility.

Non-Fund-Based Facilities

Non-fund-based facilities do not necessarily result in immediate cash disbursement. However, they create contingent obligations and consume banking exposure.

Letter of Credit (LC)

Used to support trade and procurement transactions. Can include Inland LC and Import LC.

Bank Guarantee (BG)

Issued by a bank on behalf of a company. Common categories include Financial BG and Performance BG.

The Structuring Insight — a company does not always need to increase only Cash Credit limits. Sometimes the better question is:

Is the existing mix between CC, WCDL, LC and BG aligned with the company's actual operating requirements?

An inefficient facility mix can increase interest cost, collateral requirements, limit utilisation pressure and dependency on promoter liquidity.

Drawing Power: A Sanctioned Limit Is Not Always Available Liquidity

One of the most important concepts in Indian working capital finance is Drawing Power (DP). A company may have a sanctioned Cash Credit limit. However, the amount actually available for utilisation may depend on the eligible value of underlying current assets. Typically, the calculation considers eligible inventory and book debts / receivables, after applying lender-defined margins and exclusions.

Sanctioned Limit

The maximum approved facility.

Drawing Power

The amount currently available for utilisation based on eligible current assets.

Why This Matters — a company may have a large sanctioned limit but still experience liquidity constraints if:

  • Receivables become overdue
  • Inventory becomes obsolete
  • Stock statements are delayed
  • Debtors become ineligible
  • Margins increase

The practical question for a CFO is therefore:

Is the problem really a limit shortage — or is the company losing Drawing Power?

What Do Banks Actually Evaluate Before Sanctioning or Enhancing Working Capital Limits?

Working capital lenders do not evaluate only revenue and profitability. They examine whether the underlying operating cycle supports the proposed facility.

1. Current Ratio

The Current Ratio provides an indication of the relationship between current assets and current liabilities. A traditional benchmark commonly referenced in Indian bank working capital assessment is 1.33:1 under the Tandon Committee Method II framework, although actual lender assessment can vary by transaction and policy. The lender's broader question is: does the business maintain sufficient short-term asset coverage relative to its current obligations?

2. Inventory Holding Levels

Lenders examine how long inventory remains within the operating cycle. They may ask whether inventory is aligned with production requirements, whether it has increased disproportionately, whether any inventory is slow-moving, or whether holding periods are increasing. Excess inventory can increase the working capital requirement while reducing liquidity efficiency.

3. Debtor Ageing

Receivables are not all equal from a lender's perspective. Banks may evaluate total outstanding receivables, customer-wise ageing, overdue debtors, concentration and historical collection trends. A large receivables book does not automatically translate into equivalent Drawing Power.

4. GST vs Banking Turnover

Lenders may reconcile reported business activity with banking transactions and statutory records. GST versus bank statement reconciliation is an important underwriting consideration. Inconsistencies can raise questions regarding revenue recognition, collection patterns, operating activity and data reliability.

5. CMA Data & Financial Projections

CMA data provides lenders with a structured view of historical financial performance, projected operations, working capital requirements, fund flow, cash flow and estimated borrowing requirements. The quality of projections matters — an aggressive sales projection without corresponding assumptions around inventory and receivables may not support a credible credit narrative.

6. TOL / ATNW

Lenders may assess Total Outside Liabilities / Adjusted Tangible Net Worth. This provides a perspective on overall leverage relative to the company's tangible financial base. A working capital enhancement cannot be viewed in isolation from the company's broader leverage.

7. Stock Audit Findings

For larger working capital arrangements, lenders may rely on stock audit processes. Issues around inventory records, debtor records, physical verification, ageing and reporting can affect lender confidence. The credit committee is ultimately asking: are the current assets being relied upon for lending accurately represented and adequately controlled?

8. Existing Banking Conduct

Lenders also examine how existing facilities are managed — limit utilisation, account conduct, interest servicing, submission of stock statements and covenant compliance. Working capital lending is an ongoing relationship. Historical banking conduct can therefore influence future credit decisions.

A Longer Operating Cycle Does Not Always Mean a Bigger Limit Is the Right Answer

When liquidity becomes constrained, the immediate response is often: increase the working capital limit. But additional borrowing may not address the underlying problem. Consider three possible causes.

A

Inventory Is Increasing

The business may need more capital because demand is growing. Additional limits may be justified.

B

Receivables Are Delayed

The working capital gap may be caused by weaker collection discipline. More debt may provide temporary liquidity but leave the underlying issue unresolved.

C

Supplier Credit Has Reduced

The business may be paying suppliers earlier than before. The financing structure may need to account for a permanent change in the cash conversion cycle.

EquiFin Perspective

Working capital limits should finance a healthy operating cycle — not permanently compensate for an inefficient one. Before increasing leverage, the underlying reason for the liquidity gap should be understood.

Limit Enhancement

When Does a Business Actually Need a Working Capital Limit Enhancement?

A limit enhancement may be relevant when the existing facility was structured for a smaller scale of operations. Common triggers include:

  • Revenue growth
  • Higher inventory requirements
  • Longer receivable cycles
  • New customer contracts
  • Capacity expansion
  • Seasonal demand
  • Inflation in raw material costs

However, revenue growth alone does not automatically justify a proportionate increase in borrowing limits. Lenders will generally ask:

  • Has the operating cycle changed?
  • Are receivables collectible?
  • Is inventory moving?
  • Has promoter contribution increased?
  • Can the company service higher overall debt?

The objective should be to present a limit requirement supported by operating evidence — not simply a larger funding request.

Multiple Banking & Consortium Structures

More Banking Relationships Can Solve One Problem and Create Another

As financing requirements increase, a single bank may not always be sufficient. Companies may therefore operate under multiple banking arrangements or consortium banking structures. These structures can increase funding capacity. But they can also create coordination challenges.

Common Areas of Friction

  • Different security requirements
  • Pari-passu charge creation
  • Inter-creditor coordination
  • NOCs
  • Different covenant structures
  • Limit sharing
  • Documentation timelines

Multi-banking and consortium coordination is a practical CFO concern that deserves meaningful attention.

The Advisory Challenge — the question is not simply how many banks should finance the business? It is:

Can the banking structure operate efficiently without creating unnecessary execution friction?

Collateral & Security

Working Capital Finance Is About Current Assets — But Security Still Matters

Working capital facilities are often linked to inventory, receivables and current assets. However, lenders may also seek additional security depending on borrower profile, facility size, credit quality, industry and banking policy. Security considerations can include:

  • Hypothecation of stock
  • Charge over receivables
  • Collateral property
  • Corporate guarantees
  • Personal guarantees
  • Pari-passu charge arrangements

The challenge is to understand how security creation affects future financing flexibility. A company that creates extensive collateral charges today may find future refinancing or incremental borrowing more complex.

Understand the Operating Cycle

We begin with the business, not the banking product. We analyse the procurement cycle, inventory holding, production cycle, sales cycle, receivable collection and supplier payment terms to understand where capital is actually getting locked.

02

Review Existing Banking Limits

The existing structure may include CC, OD, WCDL, LC and BG. We assess sanctioned limits, utilisation, Drawing Power, facility mix and security structure to identify whether the problem is insufficient limits, inefficient structure, Drawing Power constraints or non-fund limit pressure.

03

Assess Financial & Credit Readiness

Lenders will evaluate historical and projected financial performance — CMA data, Current Ratio, TOL / ATNW, inventory ageing, debtor ageing, banking conduct and GST reconciliation — to identify underwriting friction before entering a lender process.

Determine the Actual Working Capital Requirement

The requirement should be linked to projected turnover, operating cycle, inventory requirement, receivable days and supplier credit. This creates a more defensible financing requirement than simply requesting a larger facility.

05

Structure Fund-Based & Non-Fund-Based Limits

The financing structure may need to balance CC, WCDL, OD, LC and BG so that the banking structure supports both liquidity and commercial operations.

The lender needs to understand how the business operates, why working capital is required, what has changed, how growth affects liquidity and why the proposed facility structure is appropriate.

07

The transaction process may involve information preparation, CMA structuring, lender discussions, credit clarifications, security documentation and Conditions Precedent. Execution continues beyond sanction until the revised or new banking facilities become operational.

Important Financial Metrics

Metrics that shape working capital credit decisions:

Current Ratio

Indicates the relationship between current assets and current liabilities. A traditional Indian banking benchmark often referenced under Tandon Method II is 1.33:1, though actual credit assessment depends on lender policy and transaction specifics.

Inventory Days

Measures how long inventory remains within the operating cycle. Increasing inventory days can increase external financing requirements.

Receivable Days

Measures the time required to convert sales into cash. Longer receivable days generally increase working capital intensity.

Payable Days

Represents the period of supplier credit available to the business. Longer payable terms can reduce the immediate external funding requirement, although commercial relationships and supplier terms need to be considered.

Cash Conversion Cycle

Combines Inventory Days + Receivable Days − Payable Days. This is one of the clearest ways to understand how long operating capital remains tied up.

Drawing Power

Represents the amount currently available for utilisation under an eligible working capital facility, based on applicable lender methodology and current asset reporting.

TOL / ATNW

Provides a view of total external liabilities relative to adjusted tangible net worth. It helps lenders assess overall leverage capacity.

When Working Capital Debt May Not Be the Complete Answer

Not Every Liquidity Problem Should Be Solved With More Borrowing

Additional working capital debt may not resolve a problem where the underlying issue is:

  • Persistently overdue receivables
  • Obsolete inventory
  • Structural operating losses
  • Excessive customer concentration
  • Poor collection controls
  • Unfunded long-term capex

For example, long-term capital expenditure should generally not be permanently financed through short-term working capital borrowing. Similarly, a persistent collection problem may require operational intervention — not simply a higher Cash Credit limit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is working capital finance?

Working capital finance refers to banking facilities used to support the day-to-day operating cycle of a business. Depending on the business requirement, this can include Cash Credit, Overdraft, Working Capital Demand Loans, Letters of Credit and Bank Guarantees.

How is working capital requirement calculated?

Working capital requirements are generally linked to the operating cycle of the business, including inventory holding periods, receivable collection periods and supplier credit. Financial projections and lender-specific assessment methodologies are also relevant.

What is the difference between Cash Credit and Working Capital Demand Loan?

Cash Credit is generally structured as a revolving working capital facility subject to applicable Drawing Power and facility conditions. WCDL is a demand loan structure that may be used as part of a broader working capital arrangement. The appropriate mix depends on the transaction and lender structure.

What is Drawing Power?

Drawing Power refers to the amount currently available for utilisation under a working capital facility based on eligible current assets and applicable lender margins or exclusions. It can be lower than the total sanctioned facility limit.

What is the difference between fund-based and non-fund-based limits?

Fund-based facilities involve actual deployment of lender funds, such as Cash Credit or WCDL. Non-fund-based facilities involve contingent banking obligations, such as Letters of Credit and Bank Guarantees.

When should a company seek a working capital limit enhancement?

An enhancement may be considered when operating scale, inventory requirements, receivable cycles, seasonal demand or commercial activity has increased beyond the assumptions used for the existing banking limits.

What do banks evaluate for working capital finance?

Banks typically evaluate the company's operating cycle, financial performance, CMA data, Current Ratio, leverage, inventory and debtor ageing, banking conduct, stock reporting and consistency between financial and operational data.

Can a profitable company still face working capital problems?

Yes. Profitability and liquidity are different. A profitable business can experience cash pressure when capital remains tied up in inventory or receivables for extended periods.

Does EquiFin provide working capital loans directly?

No. EquiFin acts as an independent financial advisory firm supporting working capital structuring, lender alignment and transaction execution. Financing decisions and terms remain subject to independent lender evaluation.
