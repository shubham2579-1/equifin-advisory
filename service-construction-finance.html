Construction Finance

Construction Finance for Builders & Developers.

Construction finance funds the active building phase of a project — disbursed against progress, not upfront as a single amount. Getting the disbursement schedule, security and lender category right at this stage determines whether the project stays funded through to completion or runs into a cash gap mid-build.

Where This Becomes Relevant

Construction finance becomes the operative need once a project has cleared its approval-stage requirements and is ready to build. It typically applies when:

  • Approvals are in hand and the project can legally commence construction, but internal accruals or promoter equity alone can't fund the full build cost.
  • Sales have begun or are imminent, and the lender needs visibility on how collections will support repayment alongside the loan.
  • The developer has a track record significant enough for a lender to underwrite construction-stage risk rather than requiring the project to self-fund from sales alone.
  • A phased drawdown structure is needed rather than a lump-sum facility, so capital is released in step with actual progress and cost incurred.

What Lenders Evaluate

Construction finance is underwritten on the project's own economics, not just the developer's balance sheet. Lenders typically assess:

  • Project Cost & TEV — a technical and economic viability appraisal validating the construction budget against realistic timelines.
  • Approval Status — commencement certificate, RERA registration and any conditions attached to statutory approvals.
  • Promoter Contribution Already Infused — how much of the developer's own capital is already at risk in the project before the lender's money comes in.
  • Sales Velocity Assumptions — realistic absorption rates for the project's micro-market, not the developer's best-case projection.
  • Security & Escrow Mechanics — project mortgage, assignment of receivables and RERA-linked escrow of customer collections.
  • Developer Track Record — prior projects delivered on time, cost discipline and relationship history with lenders.

How the Financing Is Approached

Construction finance is structured as a phased facility, not a one-time disbursement. Funds are released in tranches against physical progress verified by a lender-appointed engineer — plinth, slab-wise completion, or other milestones defined in the facility agreement — with customer collections typically routed through a RERA-linked escrow account that governs how much can be withdrawn for construction versus other project costs. This protects the lender against cost or schedule overruns while giving the developer a funding line that scales with actual construction rather than a rigid upfront number that may not match how the project unfolds.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is construction finance for real estate?

Construction finance is debt structured to fund the active building phase of a real estate project, disbursed in tranches linked to construction progress rather than as a single upfront amount. It sits between land/approval-stage capital and last-mile or inventory funding in the development lifecycle.

Bank or NBFC for construction finance — which is better?

Banks generally offer lower pricing but slower, more document-intensive sanctions and stricter approval/track-record requirements. NBFCs and private credit providers move faster and are more flexible on structure, at a higher cost. The right fit depends on the developer's track record, timeline pressure and the project's approval status.

How is construction finance disbursed?

Disbursement is typically tranche-based, released against physical construction progress verified by a lender-appointed engineer, and often coordinated with an escrow mechanism for customer collections under RERA. This protects the lender against cost or schedule overruns and keeps drawdowns tied to actual project advancement.

Can construction finance be raised mid-project?

Yes, though a lender assessing a project already under construction will look closely at why financing is being raised at this stage — whether it is planned phased funding, a cost overrun, or a gap left by slower-than-expected sales — since each of these implies a different risk profile.
