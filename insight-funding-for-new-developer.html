Real Estate Funding for New Developers: Can You Raise Project Finance?

A lender's comfort with a real estate project usually leans heavily on the developer's own delivery history. A first-time developer doesn't have that history to offer — which doesn't make the project unfundable, but does mean the comfort a lender needs has to come from somewhere else.

6 Min Read·Guide·September 2026

The Question

Track record is one of the strongest signals a lender uses to underwrite real estate risk, because it's direct evidence a developer can actually deliver a project on cost, on time, and to a standard that sells. A new developer, by definition, doesn't have that evidence yet. The honest question isn't whether track record matters — it does — but what else can credibly substitute for it.

Institutional Perspective

What Can Substitute for Track Record

  • Sponsor strength: A promoter with a strong track record in an adjacent business, or significant personal net worth committed to the project, gives a lender an alternative source of comfort.
  • Experienced execution partners: Bringing in an established contractor, project management team, or JV partner with genuine delivery history can substitute for the developer's own lack of one.
  • Structural conservatism: A new developer can offer more conservative leverage, more promoter equity upfront, and tighter monitoring provisions than an established one might need to.
  • Project fundamentals: A strong location, clean title and a genuinely de-risked approval position matter more when the developer's own history can't carry part of the underwriting.

What Actually Determines Fundability

  • Who else is standing behind the project — sponsors, JV partners, or execution teams with genuine track record of their own.
  • How much promoter equity is going in — more skin in the game from a new developer offsets some of the lender's uncertainty.
  • Project fundamentals independent of the developer — location, title and approval status carry more underwriting weight.
  • Which lender category is realistic — a first project is generally NBFC or private-credit territory rather than a bank's standard product.

Questions Worth Asking

01

Does the promoter have a track record in an adjacent business, even if not real estate specifically?

02

Can an experienced contractor, PMC or JV partner be brought in to strengthen the execution story?

03

How much promoter equity can genuinely go in upfront to offset the lack of track record?

04

How strong are the project fundamentals independent of who's developing it?

EquiFin Perspective

A first project is fundable, but it needs a different underwriting story than an established developer's tenth — one built on sponsor strength, execution partnerships and project fundamentals rather than delivery history. Structuring this correctly, alongside real estate funding more broadly, is where a genuine first-time opportunity actually gets financed.

Key Takeaways

  • Lack of developer track record doesn't automatically mean a project is unfundable.
  • Sponsor strength, experienced execution partners and higher promoter equity can substitute for delivery history.
  • Project fundamentals carry more underwriting weight when the developer's own history can't.
  • A first project typically fits NBFC or private-credit lending rather than standard bank products.

Closing Perspective

The more useful question for a new developer isn't whether track record is required — it's what combination of sponsor strength, execution partnerships and project fundamentals can credibly stand in for it. That's the story a lender actually needs to hear, and it's a different story from the one an established developer tells.
