Logistics & Warehousing

A Warehouse Becomes a Financeable Asset When Location, Occupancy and Rental Cash Flow Come Together.

Logistics and warehousing assets consume capital long before they generate stable rental income — land aggregated, approvals obtained, buildings constructed, tenants secured. What Makes This Sector Different

A logistics asset is physically simple — land, civil structure, loading infrastructure — but institutional financing depends on far more: location, occupier, lease term and rental cash flow. The financing story shifts fundamentally once uncertainty is replaced by contracted occupancy: land → approvals → construction → pre-leasing → occupancy → rental cash flow, with capital sources changing at each stage (private credit for land, project debt for construction, LRD once tenanted).

Capital Requirements That Typically Arise

  • Land Aggregation — multiple parcels, sellers and title diligence, deployed before any operational cash flow exists.
  • Approvals & Development — zoning conversion and master-layout clearance before construction financing becomes available.
  • Grade-A Park / BTS Construction — civil works and infrastructure, often built around a specific occupier's requirement.
  • Tenant Fit-Out — base-building cost plus tenant-specific work ahead of occupancy.
  • Refinancing & Portfolio Financing — moving to long-term lease-backed debt once rental collections stabilise, including across multiple assets.

What Lenders Evaluate

  • Location & Connectivity — proximity to freight corridors, highways and consumption centres, and whether that relevance holds over the loan tenor.
  • Tenant Quality & Concentration — counterparty strength, balanced against the risk of depending on one or few occupiers.
  • WALE — weighted average lease expiry, showing how long contracted rental visibility actually extends.
  • DSCR & LTV — rental cash flow against debt service, and financing against asset value.
  • Vacancy & Re-Leasing Risk — what happens to debt servicing if a tenant exits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are logistics and warehousing projects typically financed in India?

Capital requirements can vary by asset stage. Early development may require land-focused capital, construction may be supported through project-oriented debt, while stabilised and occupied assets may be evaluated for long-term lease-backed financing structures.

What do lenders evaluate before financing a warehouse?

Lenders may evaluate location connectivity, land and approval status, construction requirements, tenant quality, occupancy, lease tenure, rent escalation, WALE, DSCR and asset value.

What is WALE?

WALE, or Weighted Average Lease Expiry, indicates the weighted average remaining lease duration across an asset's tenant base. It helps assess the visibility of future contracted rental cash flows.

What are the major risks in logistics and warehousing financing?

Key risks may include fragmented land acquisition, approval delays, construction delays, tenant vacancy, tenant concentration, lease expiry, connectivity challenges and changes in regional logistics patterns.
