A Credit Rating Is Not Just a Score. It Is a Credit Opinion Built on a Financial Narrative.

Companies often focus immediately on the rating outcome. For example: can we move from BBB to A? Can we avoid a downgrade? Can we negotiate lower borrowing costs? Can we improve our lender perception?

But the more important question is: what is the rating agency actually seeing when it evaluates the company? A rating outcome is generally shaped by a combination of:

  • Business position
  • Industry risk
  • Financial performance
  • Leverage
  • Debt servicing ability
  • Liquidity
  • Financial flexibility
  • Management and governance

The rating is therefore the output of a broader credit assessment process. Strategic rating advisory begins by understanding the drivers — not chasing the symbol. EquiFin explains rating-agency underwriting logic in a promoter-friendly manner, using concepts such as rating rationales, sensitivity triggers and financial risk profiling.

Signature Insight

Why Does One Company Receive a Stronger Credit Assessment Than Another?

Two businesses may have similar revenue, EBITDA and asset base. Yet their credit profiles may be materially different. Why? Because credit assessment is not based on a single ratio. Consider two companies.

Company A

  • Strong EBITDA
  • High debt
  • Weak liquidity buffer
  • Significant refinancing dependence

Company B

  • Similar EBITDA
  • Moderate leverage
  • Strong liquidity
  • Diversified funding profile

Their current profitability may appear similar. Their credit risk does not.

The Credit Assessment Difference

Profitability measures performance. Credit assessment evaluates the ability to survive financial stress.

That distinction is central to Credit Rating Advisory.

How a Credit Rating Is Built

A useful EquiFin framework:

Business Risk

Industry + Market Position + Customers

Financial Risk

Leverage + Coverage + Cash Flow

Liquidity

Cash + Banking Lines + Refinancing Capacity

Management & Governance

Strategy + Financial Discipline + Transparency

Business Risk + Financial Risk + Liquidity + Management & Governance → Credit Assessment → Rating Rationale → Rating Outcome

A company cannot meaningfully understand its rating position by looking only at one financial ratio. The rating reflects the interaction between business resilience and financial resilience.

What Does a Credit Rating Agency Actually Evaluate?

The rating committee looks at the business through multiple risk lenses.

1. Business Risk Profile

The rating analysis may consider industry characteristics, competitive position, customer concentration, geographic diversification, market share and operating track record. The question is: how resilient is the underlying business?

2. Financial Risk Profile

The agency may analyse leverage, debt structure, interest coverage, debt servicing and cash-flow generation. The question becomes: how much financial risk is the business carrying relative to its earnings and cash flows?

3. Liquidity Profile

Liquidity is often critical. The analysis may consider cash balances, unutilised banking lines, upcoming debt maturities, working capital utilisation and expected operating cash flows. The question is: can the company meet its obligations when they fall due?

4. Financial Flexibility

A company may be profitable today. But can it raise additional capital if conditions deteriorate? Financial flexibility may depend on banking relationships, funding diversification, asset monetisation capacity, equity support and market access.

5. Management & Governance

Credit analysis is not entirely mechanical. Qualitative assessment may include management track record, financial discipline, strategic decision-making, transparency and related-party transactions.

Business Risk and Financial Risk Are Different

A Strong Business Can Still Have a Weak Financial Profile

This distinction is particularly important for promoters. A company may have strong customers, excellent products, long operating history and market leadership. But if it has excessive debt and weak liquidity, the financial risk profile may constrain the overall credit assessment. The reverse can also occur — a financially conservative company may operate in a highly cyclical or competitive industry. The rating process therefore attempts to evaluate both sides.

Business Risk

How stable is the business?

Financial Risk

How resilient is the balance sheet?

Credit Strength

Can the business withstand a period when conditions are less favourable?

The Rating Rationale

The Most Important Rating Document Is Often Not the Rating Symbol

Management attention frequently goes directly to AA / A / BBB / etc. But the rating rationale can be more strategically useful. It typically explains the factors influencing the assessment.

Credit Strengths

Such as established market position, healthy operating margins, strong customer relationships and conservative leverage.

Credit Constraints

Such as high working capital intensity, customer concentration, elevated leverage, weak liquidity and refinancing dependence.

Sensitivities

Conditions that could potentially influence future rating movement.

The rating rationale therefore provides management with something valuable: an external map of how the company's credit profile is being interpreted.

Rating Sensitivity Triggers

The Future Rating Can Depend on Specific Financial Events

A rating rationale may identify conditions that could lead to:

Positive Rating Movement

For example, sustained deleveraging, stronger cash generation, improved liquidity or reduced refinancing risk.

Negative Rating Movement

For example, significant debt-funded expansion, margin deterioration, delayed collections, liquidity pressure or higher-than-expected leverage.

These are often referred to as rating sensitivities or rating triggers. The strategic value is significant. Management can ask:

Are our next business decisions moving us closer to a positive trigger or a negative trigger?

Credit Rating Is a Forward-Looking Discussion

Historical Financial Statements Explain the Past. Ratings Also Consider the Direction of Travel.

A company may currently have acceptable financial metrics. But a rating review may also consider what happens next. For example:

Current PositionDebt / EBITDA: Comfortable
Proposed DecisionLarge debt-funded capex programme
Future ImplicationLeverage could increase significantly before new capacity generates earnings

This creates a transition risk. The question is not only where is the company today? It is also what will its credit profile look like after its next major decision?

Leverage

Growth Funded by Debt Changes the Credit Equation

Debt can accelerate growth. But it also creates interest obligations, principal repayment requirements and refinancing dependence. A rating analysis therefore considers whether earnings and cash flows remain adequate relative to the debt burden. Common analytical considerations include:

Debt / EBITDA

A broad measure of leverage relative to operating earnings.

Total Outside Liabilities / Tangible Net Worth

A broader balance-sheet leverage perspective.

Net Debt

Debt after considering available cash.

No single metric determines the outcome. The quality of cash generation and business stability also matter.

Interest Coverage

Profit Is Not Enough if Financing Costs Consume the Cash Flow

Interest coverage examines the relationship between earnings and interest obligations. A company may report strong EBITDA. But if interest costs increase significantly, financial flexibility can deteriorate. The strategic questions include:

  • How much interest can the business comfortably service?
  • What happens if borrowing costs rise?
  • What happens if EBITDA declines?

Coverage analysis helps management understand the margin of safety.

Signature Insight

Liquidity

Liquidity Problems Can Develop Even in Profitable Businesses

A company can be profitable on paper while facing cash pressure. Examples: receivables increase, inventory expands, debt repayment falls due, capex consumes cash. This makes liquidity assessment critical. A rating perspective may evaluate:

Available Cash + Expected Operating Cash Flow + Unutilised Banking Facilities + Refinancing Capacity − Near-Term Obligations = Liquidity Cushion

The objective is to understand whether the company can navigate its financial obligations without excessive dependence on uncertain external events.

Refinancing Risk

Debt Does Not Disappear When the Tenor Ends

A company may have manageable interest payments today. But a large debt maturity can create a future risk. Consider:

Outstanding Debt₹200 Crore
Maturity18 Months
Internal Cash AvailableLimited

The company may depend on bank refinancing, asset sale, equity infusion or new debt. The credit question becomes: how certain is the refinancing plan? A strong credit profile generally benefits from a well-understood maturity and refinancing strategy.

The Debt Maturity Profile

A Good Balance Sheet Can Still Have a Bad Debt Calendar

Two companies may have identical total debt. But their repayment profiles may be different.

Company A

Debt maturities distributed across seven years.

Company B

Large portion of debt maturing within twelve months.

The total leverage is the same. The liquidity risk is not. Strategic finance and credit advisory should therefore examine: how much debt exists, when it falls due and how it will be repaid or refinanced.

Working Capital and Credit Risk

Operating Growth Can Create Hidden Credit Pressure

Working capital-intensive businesses can experience financial pressure despite revenue growth. For example:

Revenue Growth → Higher Receivables + Higher Inventory → Greater Working Capital Requirement → Higher Bank Utilisation → Reduced Liquidity Headroom

A rating analysis may therefore consider receivable days, inventory days, working capital utilisation, customer concentration and collection efficiency. The business may be growing. But the balance sheet may simultaneously be becoming more stretched.

Shadow Rating

Understand the Credit Position Before the Formal Rating Review

A shadow rating is not an official credit rating. It is an internal analytical exercise designed to estimate how a company's credit profile may be interpreted against relevant rating considerations. A shadow assessment may examine financial metrics, business risk, peer positioning, liquidity and rating sensitivities.

The objective is not to predict an agency outcome with certainty. It is to ask:

If the company were independently evaluated today, where might the key strengths and constraints be?

Rating Gap Analysis

The Difference Between the Current Profile and the Desired Profile

Suppose management wants to strengthen its credit position. The first question should not be how do we get a better rating? It should be what specifically is preventing the company from having a stronger credit profile today? A gap analysis may identify issues such as:

Financial Gap

Leverage higher than the desired range.

Liquidity Gap

Limited cash cushion.

Business Risk Gap

Customer concentration.

Governance Gap

Limited financial transparency.

Capital Structure Gap

Excessive short-term refinancing dependence.

Only after identifying the gap can management develop a realistic strategy.

What Can Actually Improve a Credit Profile?

Sustainable Improvement Usually Comes From the Underlying Financial Reality

There is no presentation technique that permanently substitutes for stronger credit fundamentals. Potential credit profile improvements may come from:

Deleveraging

Reducing debt relative to earnings.

Improving Cash Generation

Converting accounting earnings into operating cash flow.

Strengthening Liquidity

Building larger financial buffers and committed funding capacity.

Extending Debt Maturity

Reducing short-term refinancing pressure.

Improving Business Diversification

Reducing dependence on a single customer, geography or product.

Improving Governance Visibility

Strengthening financial reporting, internal controls and transparency.

The Core Principle

The strongest rating strategy improves the business fundamentals that the rating process is designed to evaluate.

Credit Rating and Cost of Borrowing

Why Does a Stronger Credit Profile Matter Commercially?

A credit assessment can influence how capital providers perceive risk. Potential commercial implications may include lending appetite, pricing discussions, access to institutional debt and investor confidence.

Rating transitions may influence bank borrowing spreads and financing economics, though a particular rating does not automatically guarantee a specific reduction in borrowing cost.

The Important Distinction

A stronger rating does not automatically guarantee cheaper financing. Pricing depends on market conditions, lender appetite, facility structure, security and tenor. However, a stronger and more resilient credit profile can improve the company's negotiating position across multiple sources of capital.

The BBB to A Transition Question

Why Rating Transitions Can Matter Strategically

Management may sometimes focus on crossing a particular rating category, for example BBB+ → A-. The commercial motivation may include broader lender appetite, improved market perception and potential improvement in financing discussions.

However, the strategic mistake is treating the transition as purely symbolic. The real question is:

What financial and business changes are required for the company to sustainably operate at the stronger credit profile?

Rating transition matrices help illustrate the strategic importance of understanding movement across rating categories.

Rating Transition Is a Process, Not an Event

Sustainable Credit Improvement Takes Time

A rating profile may change because of deleveraging, sustained earnings improvement, improved liquidity, better debt maturity management or stronger business diversification. These changes usually need to demonstrate consistency. A single good quarter may not redefine a company's underlying credit profile.

EquiFin Perspective

Credit strength is built through repeated financial discipline, not a single rating presentation.

The Rating Review Process

Preparation Should Begin Before the Rating Agency Meeting

Management should ideally have clarity on:

Historical Performance

What has changed?

Current Financial Position

What is the leverage and liquidity profile?

Forward Outlook

What assumptions drive the forecast?

Capital Expenditure

How will planned investments be funded?

Debt Maturities

What needs refinancing?

Rating Sensitivities

What could strengthen or weaken the profile?

The objective is not to "manage" the rating agency. It is to ensure management understands its own financial narrative before explaining it externally.

The Rating Committee Perspective

The Final Assessment Must Withstand Independent Scrutiny

A management presentation may be persuasive. But the credit case must ultimately survive independent analysis. A rating committee is likely to ask questions such as:

  • Is revenue growth sustainable?
  • Are margins recurring?
  • Is leverage increasing?
  • How strong is liquidity?
  • What happens under a downside scenario?
  • How dependent is the company on refinancing?

This is why EquiFin's advisory should focus on strengthening the underlying credit case — not simply the presentation deck.

When a Downgrade Risk Emerges

A Rating Downgrade Rarely Comes Without Earlier Warning Signals

Potential pressure indicators may include:

  • Rising leverage
  • Declining margins
  • Persistent negative cash flow
  • High working capital utilisation
  • Delayed debt repayments
  • Large refinancing requirements

The earlier management understands these trends, the more options it may have. Possible strategic responses may include deleveraging, refinancing, equity infusion, working capital optimisation or capex rationalisation.

The objective is not to react to the rating event. It is to understand the underlying financial stress before it becomes an external credit event.

Credit Rating and Capital Structure

Every Financing Decision Can Affect the Credit Profile

Consider a company evaluating:

Option A

Large additional term debt.

Option B

Moderate debt + promoter equity.

Option C

Structured financing with delayed principal amortisation.

Each option may create a different impact on leverage, interest coverage, liquidity and refinancing risk. Therefore, capital structure decisions should be evaluated not only for immediate funding availability, but also for their impact on the future credit profile. Financial Metrics That Often Matter

Credit assessment requires looking at relationships, not isolated numbers.

Debt / EBITDA

A broad measure of leverage relative to operating earnings.

Interest Coverage Ratio

Measures the relationship between earnings and interest obligations.

DSCR

Measures available cash flow relative to scheduled debt servicing.

Net Debt

Considers debt after available cash.

Current Ratio

Provides one view of short-term liquidity.

Working Capital Cycle

Shows how operating cash becomes tied up in receivables, inventory and payables.

Important EquiFin Principle — a ratio becomes meaningful only when interpreted in the context of the company's sector, business model and future capital requirements.

Sector Context Matters

The Same Financial Ratio Can Mean Different Things in Different Industries

A capital-intensive infrastructure business may naturally carry higher leverage than an asset-light technology company. A manufacturing company may require substantial working capital. A commercial real estate company may depend on contracted rental cash flows. Therefore, credit assessment cannot be reduced to universal numerical benchmarks. It requires understanding:

Sector Risk + Business Model + Capital Structure

This is where EquiFin's broader sector-specific financing perspective becomes relevant.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Credit Rating Advisory?

Credit Rating Advisory helps management understand the financial and qualitative factors that influence external credit assessment. It may include analysis of rating rationales, leverage, liquidity, coverage metrics, sensitivities and overall credit positioning.

Can an advisory firm guarantee a credit rating upgrade?

No. Credit rating decisions are independently made by the relevant credit rating agency. An advisory firm can help management understand rating drivers and strengthen strategic preparedness, but cannot guarantee a particular outcome.

What factors influence a company's credit rating?

Depending on the rating methodology and company profile, factors may include business position, industry risk, financial performance, leverage, debt servicing capacity, liquidity, refinancing risk, management quality and governance.

What is a rating rationale?

A rating rationale explains the factors supporting and constraining a credit assessment. It may also identify rating sensitivities that could influence future rating movement.

What are rating sensitivity triggers?

Rating sensitivities are factors or conditions that may potentially lead to positive or negative rating movement. Examples may include sustained deleveraging, liquidity deterioration or significant changes in operating performance.

What is a shadow rating?

A shadow rating is an internal analytical assessment of how a company's credit profile may be viewed against relevant credit considerations. It is not an official external credit rating.

Can improving a credit profile reduce borrowing costs?

A stronger credit profile can potentially improve lender confidence and financing discussions. However, actual borrowing costs depend on multiple factors including market conditions, facility structure, security, tenor and lender appetite.

How can a company avoid a credit rating downgrade?

The first step is understanding the underlying drivers of credit pressure. Depending on the situation, management may evaluate leverage reduction, liquidity strengthening, refinancing, working capital optimisation or capital structure changes.

When should a company start preparing for a rating review?

Preparation should ideally begin before the formal review process. Management should understand historical performance, current liquidity, debt maturities, forward projections and potential rating sensitivities.

Does EquiFin issue credit ratings?

No. EquiFin provides advisory support around credit analysis and rating preparedness. Credit ratings are issued independently by authorised credit rating agencies.
