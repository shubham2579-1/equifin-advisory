Every Business Has a Different Story. Its Capital Requirements Should Too.

A financing requirement is rarely solved by simply identifying a lender.

The right transaction depends on the underlying business, its cash flows, asset profile, growth plans, existing obligations and the specific purpose for which capital is required.

At EquiFin, we begin with the transaction context before evaluating the financing route.

Our Philosophy

The Right Transaction Begins With the Right Understanding.

Two businesses may require the same amount of capital for entirely different reasons.

One may require debt to complete a project. Another may need to refinance high-cost obligations. A third may be experiencing rapid growth but facing pressure on working capital.

The financing requirement may appear similar in size, but the underlying transaction reality is different.

That is why we do not begin by asking: Which lender can provide the capital?

We begin by asking: What is the business trying to achieve, and what capital structure is capable of supporting it?

Start with the business. Not the product.

Our Approach

Understand. Structure. Execute.

Our approach is built around three connected stages. Each stage informs the next.

Understand → Structure → Execute

The process is designed to move from business context to transaction strategy and ultimately to institutional execution.

01
Start with the business, not the product.

Understand

Before evaluating financing options, we seek to understand the commercial and financial context behind the requirement.

A capital requirement cannot be assessed in isolation. It needs to be viewed alongside the business model, operating cycle, existing obligations, cash-flow generation and future plans.

What We Seek to Understand

The Business — What drives revenue, profitability and cash generation?

The Requirement — What is the capital intended to achieve?

The Timing — When is the capital required, and what is driving the timeline?

The Existing Capital Structure — What debt, facilities or obligations already exist?

The Cash Flow Profile — How does the business generate and deploy cash?

The Transaction Constraints — Are there issues related to security, leverage, maturity, documentation or institutional readiness?

Business+ Cash Flows+ Existing Debt+ Capital Requirement+ Transaction Timing→ Understanding the Context

A financing requirement is not just an amount. It is a business situation that needs to be translated into a bankable transaction.

The Same Capital Requirement Can Lead to Different Financing Strategies.

01

Capacity, Equipment or Acquisition?

A manufacturing company may require ₹100 crore. But the real question is whether the capital is intended for:

  • Capacity expansion
  • Equipment acquisition
  • Working capital
  • Acquisition financing
  • Refinancing

Each scenario requires a different evaluation of cash flows, tenor, security and repayment structure.

02

Which Stage of the Project?

A real estate developer may require capital. But the underlying requirement could be:

  • Land acquisition
  • Approval-related payments
  • Premium or TDR funding
  • Construction
  • Last-mile completion
  • Refinancing

The transaction cannot be structured without understanding the project stage and cash-flow cycle.

03

Growth Without Liquidity?

A growing company may report strong revenue growth but face liquidity pressure. The issue may not be lack of profitability. It may be:

  • Receivables stretch
  • Inventory intensity
  • Rapid working capital deployment
  • Mismatch between operating and financing cycles

The objective is to identify the actual financing problem before selecting the financing solution.

02
Build the right capital strategy around the requirement.

Structure

Once the transaction context is understood, the next step is to evaluate how the requirement should be structured. This is where the financing requirement moves from a broad business objective toward a defined transaction.

The objective is not simply to maximise the amount of debt available. The objective is to develop a structure that is aligned with the purpose of capital, underlying cash flows, asset profile, repayment capacity, existing leverage and transaction timeline.

The Structuring Framework

Capital Requirement→ Purpose of Funds→ Cash Flow Profile→ Appropriate Tenor→ Repayment Structure→ Security Considerations→ Institutional Fit→ Transaction Structure

What Structuring May Involve

Capital Sizing — Determining whether the proposed requirement is aligned with the underlying transaction.

Debt Structure — Evaluating the appropriate type and combination of facilities.

Repayment Profile — Aligning repayment expectations with operating or project cash flows.

Tenor — Considering whether the financing period reflects the underlying asset or cash-generation cycle.

Security — Understanding the available collateral and transaction-specific security structure.

Capital Stack — Evaluating the relationship between equity, senior debt and other capital sources.

Refinancing Strategy — Considering whether existing facilities require replacement, restructuring or consolidation.

More capital is not always better capital. A poorly aligned facility can create pressure on the business even when the funding requirement is fully met.

A Transaction Must Make Sense From Both Sides of the Table.

A financing strategy cannot be designed solely around what the business wants. It must also address what an institutional capital provider is likely to evaluate. This requires understanding the lender’s perspective.

Depending on the transaction, capital providers may examine business model, management capability, historical financial performance, cash-flow generation, leverage, debt servicing capacity, asset quality, security, industry conditions and counterparty exposure.

03
Engage the right institutions and drive the transaction to closure.

Execute

A well-structured transaction still requires disciplined execution. Institutional financing involves multiple stages, stakeholders and documentation requirements.

Execution requires managing the process from transaction preparation through institutional engagement and subsequent documentation. The objective is to maintain clarity and momentum throughout the transaction lifecycle.

The Execution Journey

01

Transaction Preparation

Develop the information framework required to communicate the business and transaction. Depending on the requirement, this may include financial information, business overview, capital requirement, cash-flow analysis, financial model and transaction rationale.

02

Transaction Positioning

Translate the requirement into a coherent institutional proposition. The objective is to ensure the transaction narrative clearly explains why capital is required, how it will be deployed, how the structure is expected to operate and what supports repayment.

03

Institutional Mapping

Identify appropriate capital channels based on the transaction — including banks, NBFCs, HFCs, private credit providers, alternative investment funds and other relevant institutional capital providers. The objective is not broad distribution for its own sake, but identifying participants whose mandate and risk appetite may align with the transaction.

04

Institutional Engagement

Coordinate discussions and information exchange through the evaluation process. This may involve initial discussions, information sharing, management interactions, clarifications and credit queries.

05

Transaction Progression

Support the movement of the transaction through relevant stages. Depending on the transaction, this may include term discussions, structuring considerations, due diligence coordination, documentation and conditions precedent.

06

Closure & Disbursement

Support the final execution process through transaction closure. The exact path varies depending on the financing structure and institution involved.

From Business Context to Capital Execution.

The process is not necessarily linear. Transaction structures often evolve as new information emerges or institutional feedback changes the available options.

Our role is to maintain strategic clarity while navigating the execution process.

Before the Market

Institutional Engagement Is More Effective When the Transaction Is Ready.

Not every financing requirement is immediately ready to be taken to the market. Sometimes the capital requirement is clear, but the transaction framework needs further work.

Common Areas of Readiness

  • Financial information
  • Historical performance
  • Cash-flow visibility
  • Existing debt mapping
  • Security clarity
  • Project documentation
  • Capital requirement definition
  • Transaction rationale

Approaching capital providers before the transaction is properly prepared can create unnecessary friction later in the process.

Clarity in Our Role

We Do Not Start With a Funding Promise.

EquiFin Advisory is not a lender. We do not operate on the premise that every financing requirement should be taken to the market immediately.

We do not promise guaranteed funding, guaranteed approvals, guaranteed interest rates or guaranteed credit ratings.

Capital transactions involve commercial, financial and institutional considerations that must be evaluated objectively. Our role is to provide independent advisory support around capital strategy, transaction structuring and execution.

Independent advice means being able to say when a transaction needs more work before it is ready to be executed.

Better Preparation Creates Better Transaction Conversations.

The quality of an institutional financing process is influenced by the clarity of the transaction itself. A well-prepared transaction helps answer fundamental questions early: what is the capital requirement, why is it required, what will it support, what cash flows support repayment, what is the appropriate structure, what risks require explanation and which institutions may be relevant.

One Framework. Different Transaction Realities.

Corporate Finance — Focus on broader capital structure, leverage, refinancing and institutional borrowing requirements.

Project Finance — Focus on project viability, cash-flow visibility, implementation and repayment structures.

Real Estate Funding — Focus on project stage, approvals, development cash flows, sales collections and security.

Working Capital Finance — Focus on operating cycles, receivables, inventory and liquidity requirements.

Supply Chain Finance — Focus on transaction flows, anchor relationships and working capital efficiency.

Lease Rental Discounting — Focus on contracted rental cash flows, tenant quality and asset characteristics.

Strategic Finance Advisory — Focus on capital planning, financial readiness and strategic decision-making.

Credit Rating Advisory — Focus on credit positioning, rating preparedness and institutional communication.

The framework remains consistent. The analysis changes with the transaction.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does EquiFin begin with understanding the business rather than approaching lenders immediately?

Because the same capital requirement can arise from very different business situations. Understanding the underlying purpose, cash flows, existing obligations and transaction constraints helps determine whether the proposed financing structure is appropriate.

Does every financing requirement require a formal transaction process?

No. The level of preparation depends on the nature, scale and complexity of the requirement. Some transactions require detailed structuring and institutional preparation, while others may have a more straightforward execution path.

How does EquiFin identify appropriate capital providers?

Institutional engagement is evaluated based on the nature of the transaction, sector, capital requirement, structure and potential institutional fit.

Does EquiFin guarantee financing approval?

No. Financing decisions remain subject to the independent evaluation and approval processes of the relevant capital providers.

What happens if a transaction is not ready for institutional engagement?

The initial focus may need to shift toward improving transaction readiness, clarifying the capital requirement or reviewing the underlying financial and structural considerations.

Is EquiFin involved only in debt transactions?

EquiFin’s approach is centred on corporate finance, capital structuring and transaction advisory. The specific advisory scope depends on the business requirement and engagement context.
