Real Estate Funding

Capital for Every Stage of Real Estate Development.

Real estate capital needs begin long before construction starts — land acquisition, development rights, approvals, premiums and TDR can consume significant promoter capital before a project generates its first customer collection. EquiFin helps developers structure financing across the full development lifecycle, from land to construction, refinancing and asset monetisation.

Where This Becomes Relevant

Real estate development is a sequence of capital requirements, not one loan. Funding structure matters most at:

  • Land / Development Rights Acquisition — before approvals, construction progress or customer bookings exist to lean on.
  • Approvals, Premiums & TDR — concentrated capital events that lock promoter liquidity well before revenue begins.
  • Construction — capital tied to project cost, milestones and sales collections.
  • Last-Mile & Inventory Funding — bridging remaining cost or unsold stock as the project nears completion.
  • Refinancing & LRD — realigning capital once risk reduces, or moving stabilised rental assets onto long-term financing.

What Capital Providers Evaluate

Early-stage real estate can’t be underwritten on cash flows that don’t exist yet. Capital providers look instead at:

  • Legal Title — documentation, encumbrances, ownership structure and existing charges.
  • Development Potential — what can actually be built, and under what regulations.
  • Approval Visibility — status, dependencies and realistic timelines.
  • Promoter Contribution — capital already deployed and remaining commitment.
  • Exit / Take-Out Visibility — the credible path to construction finance, sales or refinancing.
  • Security & Cash-Flow Controls — charge quality, priority and escrow mechanics where applicable.

How the Financing Is Approached

Approvals, premiums and TDR are often treated as project expenses. From a financing perspective, they’re capital events — and a project can be undercapitalised before construction even begins if they consume disproportionate promoter equity. We look at the full sequence: secure the opportunity, fund approvals/premiums/TDR, transition into construction capital, move to sales-led deleveraging, then refinance or monetise the mature asset — structuring each stage so it doesn’t block financing at the next.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is land acquisition funding?

Land acquisition funding refers to capital structured for acquiring land or securing a real estate development opportunity. The appropriate structure depends on factors such as legal title, transaction structure, development potential, promoter contribution and the expected pathway towards project development or monetisation.

What is TDR funding?

TDR funding refers to capital requirements associated with acquiring Transferable Development Rights where applicable to a real estate development. The transaction should generally be evaluated in the context of the project's development potential, overall economics and capital structure.

Why is early-stage real estate funding more difficult?

At the land and approval stage, a project may not yet have construction progress, customer bookings or operating cash flows. Capital providers therefore rely more heavily on legal clarity, development potential, approval visibility, promoter commitment and the future pathway towards project monetisation.

Does EquiFin provide real estate loans directly?

No. EquiFin acts as an independent advisory firm supporting capital structuring and transaction execution. Financing decisions, terms and approvals remain subject to the independent processes of relevant lenders and capital providers.
