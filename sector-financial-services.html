Financial Services & Fintech

In Financial Services, Capital Does Not Just Support Growth. It Enables the Business Model.

For lenders and financial platforms, access to capital directly determines the ability to originate, hold and grow financial assets. But larger borrowing capacity alone does not create a stronger institution.

Growth must remain supported by adequate capital, disciplined underwriting, asset quality, collection performance and a liability structure capable of funding the maturity profile of the loan book. EquiFin helps financial services businesses evaluate institutional capital requirements through the lens of balance-sheet capacity, loan-book quality, ALM discipline, liquidity and sustainable leverage.

For Financial Institutions, the Balance Sheet Is the Operating Platform

A conventional business uses capital to build operations. A financial institution uses capital to create financial assets.

Equity / Net Worth → Borrowing Capacity → Liability Base → Loan or Financial Asset Creation → Interest / Fee Income → Collections → Liquidity → Redeployment

This cycle means that growth and funding are inseparable. A financial services business can have strong demand for credit products and still be constrained by its ability to fund the balance sheet responsibly.

The Financial Services Capital Landscape

Growth Requires More Than One Source of Capital

A broad institutional financing toolkit for NBFCs and financial platforms includes bank term loans, WCDL, NCDs, securitisation, Pass-Through Certificates, Direct Assignment, co-lending, ECB and private credit. Different sources solve different balance-sheet requirements.

Equity

Loss-absorbing capital.

Bank Borrowings

Core liability funding.

NCDs

Institutional debt diversification.

Securitisation / DA

Capital recycling.

Co-Lending

Balance-sheet optimisation.

Private Credit / Structured Capital

Special situations / growth.

EquiFin Perspective

The objective is not simply to maximise borrowing. It is to build a liability structure that can support asset growth without creating excessive concentration, liquidity or refinancing risk.

Signature Insight

The Financial Institution Capital Cycle

Equity Capital → Institutional Borrowing → Liability Deployment → Loan Originations → Loan Book Growth → Interest Income → Collections → Liquidity Recovery → Capital Redeployment

Unlike many industries, capital is continuously recycled through the balance sheet. The strength of this cycle depends on whether assets perform as expected, collections remain stable, liabilities remain available, and maturities remain aligned.

Where Financial Services Businesses Typically Need Capital

Capital requirements are directly linked to asset growth and liability management.

01

On-Lending Capital

For lenders, debt capital may be required to fund retail loan originations, MSME lending, secured lending, vehicle finance, housing-linked lending, supply chain financing and other credit portfolios. The core question is: how much incremental asset growth can the institution support with its current equity base and liability structure?

02

Loan Book Growth

A growing loan book requires a growing liability base:

New Borrowing → New Loan Originations → Asset Book Growth → Interest Income → Collections

However, growth must be evaluated against capital adequacy, asset quality, leverage and liquidity.

03

Liability Diversification

Over-reliance on one funding source can create institutional risk. A financial institution may seek to diversify across banks, NBFC lenders, debt capital markets, securitisation investors, co-lending partners and private credit providers, reducing dependence on any single liability provider.

04

Term Debt and WCDL

Bank term loans and WCDL are relevant financing structures for the sector. These facilities may support loan-book funding, liability diversification and liquidity planning. The structure must be evaluated against the maturity profile of the underlying assets.

05

NCD Issuance

Non-Convertible Debentures can provide an institutional debt avenue for eligible financial institutions — diversifying liabilities, accessing institutional investors and extending borrowing maturity. But NCD readiness generally requires a stronger institutional credit proposition.

06

Securitisation

Securitisation can allow a lender to monetise pools of financial assets:

Loan Book → Identify Asset Pool → Structure Transaction → Transfer / Securitise Cash Flows → Capital Recovery → Redeployment

Securitisation and PTC structures are important capital sources for financial services businesses.

07

Direct Assignment

Direct Assignment can provide another mechanism for transferring pools of loans to institutional counterparties. For an originator, the strategic relevance may include liquidity generation, balance-sheet recycling and asset concentration management.

08

Co-Lending Structures

Co-lending is a relevant structure for the sector:

Origination Platform + Institutional Funding Partner → Coordinated Credit Deployment

The structure can help financial platforms combine origination capability with external institutional balance-sheet capacity.

09

Growth Capital

Rapidly scaling financial institutions may require additional capital to support net worth, regulatory capital buffers and balance-sheet growth. The financing challenge is often not simply raising debt — it is ensuring that equity and debt capacity grow in a sustainable relationship.

10

Refinancing and Liability Optimisation

A growing institution may accumulate multiple facilities with different tenors, pricing, covenants and security structures. Over time, the capital structure may require rationalisation.

The Core Financial Services Financing Challenge

Loan Book Growth Can Outpace Institutional Funding Capacity

The growth cycle can appear straightforward:

More Capital → More Loans → More Interest Income → More Profit

But financial institutions operate with additional constraints:

More Loans → Higher Risk Exposure → Greater Capital Requirement → Larger Liability Base → Greater ALM Complexity

Core EquiFin Principle

For a financial institution, growth is sustainable only when asset expansion remains proportionate to capital strength, funding availability and risk-management capacity.

Signature Insight

The Financial Services Capital Equation

Borrowing Capacity Is Not the Same as Sustainable Balance-Sheet Capacity

Net Worth → Capital Adequacy → Borrowing Capacity → Liability Base → Asset Book Growth → Asset Quality → Collection Performance → Liquidity

A weakness at any stage can affect the entire funding cycle.

Signature Insight

How Lenders Underwrite Financial Services Businesses

Core underwriting parameters include capital adequacy, GNPA / NNPA, leverage, ALM and liquidity, collection efficiency, promoter / parentage, and credit rating. Unlike conventional corporate lending, the lender is evaluating both the financial institution as a borrower and the quality of the financial assets created using borrowed funds.

Signature Insight

Capital Adequacy: The Foundation of Institutional Growth

Equity Creates the Capacity to Absorb Risk

For a financial institution, net worth is more than an accounting metric. It supports loss absorption, balance-sheet expansion and institutional confidence.

Equity / Net Worth → Risk-Absorbing Capacity → Ability to Support Assets → Institutional Borrowing Capacity

Core Question

Can the institution continue growing its financial assets without stretching its capital base?

Loan Book Growth vs Capital Growth

Asset Growth Should Not Be Viewed Independently From Net Worth Growth

Loan Book Growth ↑ → Risk Exposure ↑ → Capital Requirement ↑

An institution growing rapidly may require periodic strengthening of equity, retained earnings and capital buffers.

EquiFin Perspective

The strongest funding strategy connects loan-book growth plans with the capital required to support that growth — not simply with the debt available in the market.

Signature Insight

Asset Quality: The Second Half of the Funding Story

A Growing Loan Book Is Valuable Only If It Continues to Perform

GNPA and NNPA are central underwriting metrics. The lender's perspective is not only how fast the book is growing, but what is happening to the quality of the book as it grows.

Loan Origination → Repayment Behaviour → Delinquency → NPA Formation → Credit Cost → Net Worth Impact

Asset quality ultimately influences profitability, liquidity, capital adequacy and future borrowing capacity.

GNPA and NNPA: Why Both Matter

Asset Quality Must Be Viewed Before and After Credit Protection

GNPA

Provides a view of gross stressed assets.

NNPA

Reflects stressed assets after applicable provisioning adjustments.

Higher Delinquency → Higher Credit Cost → Lower Profitability → Pressure on Net Worth → Pressure on Future Growth

EquiFin Perspective

For lenders to financial institutions, asset quality is not a historical reporting metric. It is a forward-looking indicator of capital preservation and future funding capacity.

Signature Insight

Collection Efficiency: Where Credit Quality Becomes Cash Flow

Loan Assets Create Value Only When Scheduled Cash Flows Are Collected

Collection efficiency is a core sector metric.

Loan Disbursement → Repayment Schedule → Collection → Liquidity → Debt Repayment → Redeployment

A lender may therefore evaluate collection trends, delinquency buckets, recovery performance and historical portfolio behaviour.

Core Insight

In lending businesses, collections are the operating cash flow.

Collection Performance and Liability Servicing

Asset Cash Flows Must Support Liability Obligations

The financial institution operates between two schedules:

Asset Side (Customer Repayments) → Liability Side (Institutional Borrowings)

If collections weaken, the institution may face pressure even before the full economic loss is recognised. This is why collection performance affects liquidity, ALM and refinancing capacity.

Signature Insight

Leverage: Growth Accelerator or Balance-Sheet Risk?

Leverage, including borrowings relative to net worth, is a core sector metric.

Total Borrowings ÷ Net Worth = Leverage

Higher leverage can support faster asset growth. But it also reduces the institution's capacity to absorb asset-quality deterioration, collection disruption and funding volatility.

Core Question

Is leverage being supported by demonstrated asset quality and adequate liquidity?

Signature Insight

Asset-Liability Management: The Central Liquidity Discipline

A Profitable Loan Book Can Still Create Stress If Asset and Liability Maturities Are Misaligned

Longer-Duration Loans → Funded By → Shorter-Duration Borrowings

This creates refinancing dependence. The financial institution must continually replace maturing liabilities. ALM analysis therefore examines asset maturity, liability maturity, cash-flow timing and liquidity gaps.

EquiFin Perspective

Profitability does not eliminate refinancing risk. Maturity mismatch must be understood independently.

The ALM Gap

The Timing of Cash Inflows and Outflows Matters as Much as Their Total Value

Asset Cash Inflows vs Liability Cash Outflows → Maturity Gap → Liquidity Requirement

A well-capitalised institution may still face stress if liabilities mature faster than collections arrive, funding sources become unavailable, or asset pools cannot be monetised quickly.

Signature Insight

Liquidity Is Different From Profitability

A Financial Institution Can Be Profitable and Still Face Liquidity Pressure

This distinction is critical. Profitability asks: is the institution economically earning money? Liquidity asks: can the institution meet obligations when they fall due?

Profitability ≠ Liquidity

The funding strategy must therefore consider both.

Funding Concentration: The Hidden Balance-Sheet Risk

Dependence on a Small Number of Lenders Can Limit Strategic Flexibility

An institution may have adequate current borrowing. But if a large portion comes from one bank, one NBFC or one investor category, the business can become vulnerable to changes in lender appetite.

Core Insight

Funding diversification is not only about raising more debt. It is about reducing dependence on any single source of liquidity.

Bank Borrowing vs Capital Market Debt

Different Liability Sources Create Different Institutional Requirements

Bank Funding

Potential strengths: relationship-led, flexible facility structures, established institutional route.

NCD / Market Debt

Potential strengths: broader institutional investor access, potential liability diversification. But may require stronger governance, credit positioning, rating profile and institutional disclosures.

EquiFin Perspective

The appropriate funding source depends on the institution's stage of maturity and readiness — not simply the availability of capital.

Signature Insight

Securitisation: Capital Recycling for a Mature Loan Book

A Performing Asset Pool Can Become a Source of Fresh Liquidity

Originate Loans → Build Performing Pool → Identify Eligible Assets → Securitise / Transfer → Generate Liquidity → Originate Again

Securitisation, PTC and Direct Assignment are key financing structures.

Core Strategic Benefit

The transaction can help convert a portion of the existing loan book into liquidity for further deployment, subject to transaction-specific structure and eligibility.

Co-Lending: Origination Capability Meets Institutional Balance Sheet

Not Every Growing Credit Platform Needs to Hold Every Asset Fully on Its Own Balance Sheet

Origination + Customer Access + Underwriting Capability → Partnered With → Larger Funding Capacity

The strategic value lies in separating origination capability from balance-sheet capacity.

Key Question

Can the institution continue scaling credit origination without proportionately increasing its own funding requirement?

Fintech: Technology Does Not Remove Balance-Sheet Discipline

Digital Origination Can Accelerate Growth. It Can Also Accelerate Risk.

Technology-led lending platforms may demonstrate faster customer acquisition, digital underwriting, automated collections and data-driven credit models. But institutional funding still requires confidence in credit quality, portfolio performance, collection outcomes, governance and risk controls.

EquiFin Perspective

Technology may improve origination efficiency, but institutional capital is ultimately underwritten against the quality and behaviour of the financial assets created.

Rapid Loan Book Growth: When Does Growth Become a Risk?

High Growth Can Reduce Credit Visibility

New Originations → Limited Vintage History → Uncertain Long-Term Performance

A newly originated portfolio may initially appear healthy simply because it has not yet seasoned. The underwriting process may therefore consider vintage performance, cohort behaviour, delinquency migration and collection trends.

Core Insight

For financial institutions, the quality of growth often becomes visible only after the portfolio has seasoned.

Vintage Performance

Portfolio Quality Should Be Evaluated Across Origination Cohorts

Rather than viewing the entire book as one number, a lender may analyse each vintage year's performance separately, to identify whether newer growth cohorts are behaving differently from historical portfolios.

Signature Insight

Promoter, Management and Governance

Promoter and parentage strength are important underwriting considerations. For financial institutions, governance has direct credit relevance. Lenders may consider management experience, credit discipline, governance frameworks, risk oversight and institutional controls.

Core Question

Can the institution maintain underwriting and collection discipline as the balance sheet scales?

Credit Rating and Institutional Funding Capacity

External Credit Positioning Can Influence Access to Capital

Credit rating is a core consideration in financial-services underwriting. A stronger institutional credit profile can support engagement with banks, debt investors and institutional counterparties. The relevant issue is not merely obtaining a rating — it is understanding what drives the rating, which financial metrics create sensitivity, and how growth and leverage affect the institutional credit narrative.

Financial Services Sector Risks

Growth and Funding Must Be Evaluated Together

Asset Quality Deterioration

Weakening collections can increase credit costs and reduce net worth.

Liquidity Risk

Maturing liabilities may create pressure before corresponding asset cash flows are realised.

ALM Mismatch

Short-term funding supporting longer-duration assets can create refinancing dependence.

Funding Concentration

Dependence on limited lenders can increase vulnerability.

Excessive Leverage

High borrowings relative to net worth can reduce resilience.

Rapid Portfolio Growth

New loan vintages may not yet demonstrate long-term performance.

Collection Disruption

Weak collection performance directly affects liquidity.

Governance Weakness

Weak risk controls can undermine institutional confidence.

The Financial Services Capital Structure

A Strong Institution Requires Multiple Layers of Capital

Equity / Net Worth → Risk-Absorbing Capital → Senior Institutional Debt → Term Loans / WCDL / NCDs → Asset-Backed Liquidity → Securitisation / DA / PTC → Partnered Capital → Co-Lending

Each layer has a different role. The objective is to avoid overdependence on a single liability structure.

The Financial Institution Funding Waterfall

Equity → Creates Capital Base → Borrowing → Funds Asset Growth → Loan Collections → Generate Cash Inflows → Securitisation → Recycles Capital → Retained Profits → Strengthen Net Worth → Stronger Net Worth → Supports Future Growth

This is the compounding cycle of a mature financial institution.

When Financial Services Capital Advisory May Be Relevant

Consider a capital discussion when:

  • The loan book is growing faster than available liabilities
  • Funding is concentrated among a limited number of lenders
  • Existing facilities require refinancing
  • The institution wants to diversify beyond traditional bank debt
  • A performing loan pool may be suitable for securitisation
  • Co-lending opportunities are being evaluated
  • ALM gaps are increasing

  • Institutional investors require a stronger credit narrative
  • Management wants to align growth targets with sustainable leverage and liquidity

Frequently Asked Questions

How do NBFCs raise institutional capital?

Structures can include bank term loans, WCDL, NCDs, securitisation, PTCs, direct assignment, co-lending, ECB and private credit, depending on the institution and transaction requirements.

What do lenders evaluate before lending to an NBFC or financial institution?

Core considerations include capital adequacy, asset quality, GNPA/NNPA, leverage, ALM, liquidity, collection efficiency, promoter or parentage strength and credit positioning.

Why is capital adequacy important for financial institutions?

The capital base provides loss-absorbing capacity and supports the institution's ability to grow risk assets and maintain institutional confidence.

Why is collection efficiency important?

Collections convert financial assets into actual cash inflows. Weak collection performance can affect liquidity, debt servicing capacity and future borrowing ability.

What is the difference between asset quality and liquidity?

Asset quality evaluates the performance of the loan book. Liquidity evaluates whether the institution can meet its obligations when they fall due. A business can be profitable yet face liquidity pressure because of timing mismatches.

What is ALM and why does it matter?

Asset-Liability Management examines the relationship between the maturity and cash-flow profiles of assets and liabilities. Significant mismatches can create refinancing and liquidity risk.

Can securitisation help an NBFC grow?

Securitisation can provide a mechanism to monetise eligible pools of financial assets and recycle liquidity, subject to the specific transaction structure and applicable requirements.

What is co-lending from a capital perspective?

Co-lending can combine the origination capability of one institution with the funding capacity of another institutional partner, depending on the applicable structure and partnership model.
